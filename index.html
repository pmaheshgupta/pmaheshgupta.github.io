About

I build software for finance, where a bug can mean lost money and any decision might one day have to be explained to a regulator. That shapes how I think about AI. I'm genuinely excited by how much of the work it can take on, and I always want to know who is accountable when it does.

The problem I keep coming back to is rules locked inside documents: pricing rules, fund agreements, regulations. People write them in prose, and someone has to turn them into something a system can execute and prove is right. I first chased it with a hand-built compiler. Now I chase it with agents. The tools changed; the question didn't.

What I believe

  • Humans move up, not out.AI-first means people spend their time on judgment, specification and verification.
  • Measure, don't feel.Developers can feel 20% faster while being 19% slower. Cycle time and failure rates settle it.
  • Brakes before engine.Rollback, enforced gates and named owners come before more autonomy.
